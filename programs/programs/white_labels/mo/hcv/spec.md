   **Income counted for this test is § 5.609(b)-adjusted, not raw gross.** Four exclusions are applied per member. § 5.609(b) is a **union, not a sequence** — its lead-in reads "Annual income does not include the following", so an amount (b)(3) excludes cannot be re-included by (b)(14), which excludes amounts and never includes one. That union is what fixes limb 2's scope below: (b)(3) takes every under-18 earner who is not the head or spouse in full, and what is left for (b)(14) is the dependent full-time student aged 18 or over. **The regulation sets no age on (b)(14) itself**, so an implementation that encodes that residue by **branch order** rather than by an age test — which is what `ks_hcv` does, its `_is_minor` branch returning first and neither branch carrying an age comparison (`programs/programs/white_labels/ks/hcv/calculator.py`, `KsHcv._countable_earned_income`, benefits-api `origin/main`) — must keep the minor branch first, or a member who is both under 18 and a full-time student has $480 of their wages counted instead of none. Scenario 31 pins that member; no other scenario contains one.
   1. Earned income of a member under 18 **who is not the head of household or spouse** — excluded in full, uncapped (§ 5.609(b)(3)). The carve-out is not a refinement: § 5.609(a)(1) includes all amounts received by each member who is 18 or over **or** is the head of household or spouse of the head of household, and (b)(3)'s exclusion is scoped to "children", not to members. A minor head or spouse therefore contributes their earned income in full. `ks_hcv` carries the same carve-out and cites the same provision for it (`programs/programs/white_labels/ks/hcv/calculator.py`, `KsHcv._countable_earned_income`, benefits-api `origin/main`). The population is enterable — the frontend's birth-year validator sets no minimum age for the head, only a maximum age and the current year (`src/Components/Steps/HouseholdMembers/utils/schema.ts`, the `birthYear` validator, benefits-calculator `origin/main`) — and Scenario 26 pins it.
   2. Earned income of a dependent full-time student aged 18 or over, above the § 5.611 dependent-deduction amount (§ 5.609(b)(14)).
   3. Income reported under `workersComp` (§ 5.609(b)(5)).
   4. All remaining income of a member whose `relationship` is `fosterChild` (§ 5.609(b)(8)).

   § 5.609(b)(4) is **not** in this list. It excludes "Payments received for the care of foster children or foster adults" — money received by the caregiver, so income of the head or spouse, not of the foster child. The screener has no income type for such a payment, so it cannot be identified and separated from the caregiver's other income; it is recorded in Missing Eligibility Criteria 6 with the other exclusions the income taxonomy cannot reach. Applying it to the foster child's own income instead would do nothing, since (b)(8) already excludes that in full.
   - Evaluation scope: `member`
   - Captured via: `birth_year_month` (`HouseholdMember`, date) via `calc_age()` against the pinned reference date; `student_full_time` (`HouseholdMember`, bool); `relationship` (`HouseholdMember`, str); accessor `member.calc_gross_income("yearly", ["earned"|"unearned"], exclude=self.EXCLUDED_INCOME_TYPES)`, the two-call pattern `ks_hcv` uses at `calculator.py`, `KsHcv._annual_income`. `EARNED_INCOME_TYPES` is a module constant — `frozenset(("wages", "selfEmployment"))` at `screener/models.py` — and is what `"earned"` resolves to inside `calc_gross_income`. `EXCLUDED_INCOME_TYPES` is **not** shared: each HCV calculator defines its own class attribute, and both `il_hcv` and `ks_hcv` set their `EXCLUDED_INCOME_TYPES` to `("workersComp",)`. Missouri should do the same, which is what implements § 5.609(b)(5). All at benefits-api `origin/main`.
   - Implementation note: must aggregate per member, never a single `Screen.calc_gross_income(..., exclude=[...])` — one shared exclude list strips income from the wrong members. Excluding a member's income never removes them from `household_size`, the dependent count or the bedroom lookup.

3. **Assistance is restricted to citizens and noncitizens with eligible immigration status; a mixed family with at least one eligible member may still be assisted.**

   - Implementation note: scoped at `config` so a household selecting only ineligible statuses is excluded outright rather than prorated toward $0. 4. **The household is not already receiving Housing Choice Voucher assistance.**
   - Evaluation scope: `household`
   - Captured via: accessor `Screen.has_base_benefit("section_8")`, backed by `CurrentBenefit` (screen ↔ program join)
   - Implementation note: this is MFB's non-duplication convention — a household already holding the benefit is not shown as eligible for it — rather than a distinct federal eligibility bar; 24 CFR 982.201(b)(1)(ii) treats a household continuously assisted under the 1937 Housing Act as a separate admission category rather than an exclusion, so the sourced concept is adjacent rather than identical. The accessor keys on the program's `base_program` value, not the program key. It returns `False` for every Missouri screen unless **three** conditions hold on the `mo_hcv` program row: `base_program: "section_8"`, `show_in_has_benefits_step: true`, and `active: true`. Two of the three come from the has-benefits queryset, which filters `active=True, show_in_has_benefits_step=True, white_label__code=...` (`screener/views.py`, `HasBenefitsProgramsView.get`); `base_program` is not in that filter — it is what `Screen.has_base_benefit` matches on (`screener/models.py`, `Screen.has_base_benefit` — `base_program in self._current_benefit_base_programs`). Both at benefits-api `origin/main`. The accompanying config sets the first two and deliberately ships `active: false`, which is correct for a seed config and means this criterion is inert until the program is switched on. Switching it on is an Implement step, not a config defect. This criterion is a second safeguard, not the only one: the results layer already suppresses a program the household reports holding, setting `already_has` from `screen.has_benefit(program.name_abbreviated)` **after** both calculator methods have run (`screener/views.py`, `eligibility_results`) and filtering on `!program.already_has` in the frontend (`src/Components/Results/Filter/filterPrograms.ts`, `isProgramBasicallyVisible`, benefits-calculator `origin/main`) — the route the shipped KS and IL HCV specs both name as the reason they carry no such criterion. That route keys on `name_abbreviated` where this one keys on `base_program`; the two coincide in Missouri because no other Missouri program carries `base_program: "section_8"`, checked across all 27 shipped Missouri seed configs (`programs/management/commands/import_program_config_data/data/`, benefits-api `origin/main`). Evaluating the gate in the calculator as well is deliberate, and it does not disturb the current-benefits listing, which is filtered separately on `active` and `show_on_current_benefits` (`programs/serializers.py`).
   - Source (contrast, not support): 24 CFR 982.201(d)(1). This criterion is MFB's product convention and has **no** federal source; the provision below is given only so a reader can see what the federal rule does with the same facts, which is to make continuous assistance an admission route *in* rather than a bar. No shipped HCV sibling implements this criterion — `has_base_benefit` appears in none of the four calculators on `origin/main`. If a sourced basis is ever wanted, 24 CFR 982.352(c) on concurrent subsidies is the provision to capture; it is not in this package's source set. — "An applicant is continuously assisted under the 1937 Housing Act if the family is already receiving assistance under any 1937 Housing Act program when the family is admitted to the voucher program." — [snapshot `2026-09-28--24-cfr-982-201`](../../../sources/mo/mo_hcv/2026-09-28--24-cfr-982-201/content.md), accessed 2026-09-28

## Missing Eligibility Criteria (Data Gaps)

1. **Five of the six § 982.201(b)(1) admission categories reach households above the very-low-income limit.**
   - Why: (ii) continuous 1937-Act assistance, (iii) PHA-adopted categories, (iv) HOPE 1/2 non-purchasing status, (v) displacement by mortgage prepayment or FHA termination, and (vi) § 248.173 non-purchasing status each turn on a fact no screener field captures. (iii) is closed **at HAKC** by that agency's own policy (Covered Criterion 1), which is the one agency whose plan was read; whether any of the other 44 has adopted additional categories is unknown, and one that had would admit households this spec screens out. A household already reporting Section 8 is suppressed by Covered Criterion 4 before this calculator's income test runs, so (ii) is additionally unobservable.
   - Handling: `assumed-met` — only the very-low-income gate is applied, so a household admissible under one of these five categories at a higher income is screened out. Narrows results. Not surfaced in the description: a narrowing gap screens the household out before any description is shown, so no description text could reach the affected user.

3. **A student enrolled in higher education, under 24, unmarried, without a dependent child and not a veteran is barred from Section 8 assistance unless disabled, assisted as of 30 November 2005, individually income-eligible, or with income-eligible parents.**
   - Why: § 5.612 is seven conjunctive conditions, (a)–(g). Conditions (f) and (g) turn on disability-plus-2005-assistance history and parental income eligibility — neither is collected. `student`, `student_full_time` exist but cannot establish (f) or (g).
   - Handling: `assumed-met` — no student gate is applied at all, rather than a partial one. Widens results. Surfaced in the description, and named rather than left to the general catch-all: it says "Extra rules may also apply if you are a student." This is the one widening gap that changes the **answer** rather than the estimate — § 5.612 is a categorical statutory bar, so a household shown eligible here may not qualify at all, where every other unsurfaced widening gap only skews the dollar figure. Stated at the level a household can act on, without enumerating the seven conditions. **It clears the breadth gate on consequence, not on reach**: the population is narrow — enrolled in higher education, under 24, unmarried, no dependent child, not a veteran and not otherwise exempt — but what it faces is a categorical statutory bar, not a skewed estimate, and every caveat in a description is read by every household that sees the card. With this one the description carries **three** caveats — a background check (Gap 2), an asset or homeownership limit (Gaps 4 and 9, which share a single clause and so cost no extra words), and student rules — which is the budget, not room to spare. Because HAP is a *difference*, the effect on an estimate is far larger than ten percent. **It does not reach every value, though.** Of the thirty-three scenarios at ZIP 64130, four are ineligible and two more do not move at all: Scenario 9's reported rent is below the standard, so `min(payment_standard, gross_rent)` returns rent whichever standard applies, and Scenario 2's TTP already exceeds both, so it floors at $1 either way. That direction is conservative, not neutral, in the same way the $50 minimum rent is. 16. **Three of the five exceptions in HAKC's subsidy standard turn on facts the screener does not hold.**
    - Why: the standard is one bedroom per two persons *except* for five circumstances (Benefit Value, "Bedroom size"). Two of the five are handled and three are not. The generations exception is modelled, because it is derivable from `relationship`; the single-person exception needs no separate handling, because the base ratio already yields one bedroom for one person. The opposite-sex exception and its under-5 carve-out need each member's sex, and `HouseholdMember` carries no sex or gender field at all (`screener/models.py`, `HouseholdMember`, benefits-api `origin/main`). The live-in-aide bedroom needs a live-in aide to be identifiable, and no `relationship` value names one (Missing Eligibility Criteria 6). Separately, four inputs carry no generation of their own: `relatedOther` — "aunt, uncle, cousin, in-law, etc." — genuinely spans generations, `roommate` and `boyfriendOrGirlfriend` state a living arrangement rather than a kinship distance, and a null or unrecognised `relationship` states nothing at all — the field is nullable (`screener/models.py`, `HouseholdMember.relationship`, benefits-api `origin/main`) and the serializer does not require it.

Not surfaced in the description: the household is shown eligible with a somewhat low estimate, and no wording about which household members HUD would house separately would change what they should do next.

17. **The family unit size is capped at four bedrooms, which is MFB's limit rather than HAKC's.**
    - Why: the per-generation split can sum above four in any household large enough to spread across occupied generations, which does **not** require five of them. ## Benefit Value

- Value: `max(1, monthly HAP × 12)` where monthly HAP = `max(0, min(payment_standard, gross_rent_proxy) − TTP)` — an estimated prospective subsidy, not a predicted HAP for a specific unit
- `value_format`: `estimated_annual` (annualized)
- Variation axes: county/ZIP payment standard · bedroom size (household size) · household income · dependent count · elderly-or-disabled family status · reported medical expenses · reported childcare expenses · reported rent — each appears in Test Scenarios
- Justification: the PHA pays the Housing Assistance Payment directly to the landlord each month, so the household's benefit is the annual monetary equivalent of that payment. The payment standard is modelled at 100% of the applicable FMR or SAFMR — a declared assumption that the one Missouri agency with a published schedule exceeds; see "Payment standard" below and Missing Eligibility Criteria 14.

**Payment standard.** 100% of the FY2026 FMR, or of the ZIP-level Small Area FMR where HUD marks the metro mandatory-SAFMR. Kansas City, MO-KS HMFA and St. Louis, MO-IL HMFA carry the FY2026 schedule's `+` marker, which that schedule's note defines as the mandatory-SAFMR flag; Springfield, MO HMFA and Columbia, MO HMFA do not and use the area-wide FMR; non-metro counties use the county FMR. **The 100% is an assumption this spec declares, and it is low for the largest agency named here.** A PHA may set its standard anywhere in HUD's 90–110 percent basic range without HUD approval. HAKC's plan gives the range without saying where HAKC sits — its "100 percent of FMR" is HUD's illustrative example, not HAKC's schedule — but does establish the two things that would put HAKC *outside* it: no exception payment standards and no designated payment standard areas. HAKC's website settles the rest, at the top of the range: its published per-ZIP schedule reproduces the SAFMR workbook's `110% Payment Standard` column exactly, at every bedroom size (Missing Eligibility Criteria 14). The 100% is kept because it is what the shared client returns for every white label and a Missouri-only schedule has nowhere to live. A value below is therefore understated wherever the administering agency sits above 100% **and** the payment standard governs that household's HAP — by more than ten percent of the estimate, since HAP is a difference. Where reported rent falls below the standard, or TTP already exceeds it, the standard's level never reaches the value (Scenarios 9 and 2).

**St. Louis City is a county-equivalent, and until MFB-2110 lands the state's second-largest voucher market returns $0.** Missouri is the first HCV white label containing an independent city. `Screen.county` for the 27 St. Louis-city ZIPs stores the literal `"St. Louis City"` (`configuration/white_labels/mo.py`, from the `"63101"` entry onward — `"63101": {"St. Louis City": "St. Louis City"}` — with `"St. Louis County"` a separate value in the same file). `_get_entity_id` appends a suffix to anything not already ending in `county`:

```python
county_name = county_name.strip()
if not county_name.lower().endswith("county"):

```

so the lookup string becomes `"St. Louis City County"`, which is compared by exact case-insensitive equality against HUD's `fmr/listCounties/MO` and matches nothing. The result is `HudIncomeClientError`, which both `household_eligible` and `household_value` must catch — so every St. Louis-city household presents as **not eligible, $0**: the same correct-looking wrong answer this spec flags for household sizes above 8. It is deterministic, not intermittent, and both accessors fail together, because `get_screen_il_ami` and `get_screen_payment_standard` each resolve the county through `_get_entity_id` (`integrations/clients/hud_income_limits/client.py`, `_get_entity_id`; the FMR path via `get_screen_payment_standard`). All read at benefits-api `origin/main`.

**The fix is MFB-2110, not a Missouri-specific override.** MFB-2110 changes `_get_entity_id` to handle a county name that does not end in "County". `MoHcv` then needs **no** special-casing — it passes `screen.county` like every other Missouri household, and `"St. Louis City"` resolves on its own because the county match is already case-insensitive (`name.lower() == county_name.lower()`, in `client.py`'s `_get_entity_id`) and HUD spells it `"St. Louis city"`. Do **not** add a `county_override`: `mo_hcv` serves all 115 Missouri counties, and the fixed-class-attribute shape `MaCha` uses is safe only because it gates on one city — a fixed Missouri override would look up every Missouri household as though it were in St. Louis. `"St. Louis City"` is the only stored county value ending in `" city"` across all nine white labels and it fails 100 percent of the time today, so the fix regresses nothing.
**A county split between two HUD areas still resolves uniquely, so no disambiguation is needed.** Crawford County appears in both datasets MFB reads — the FY2026 FMR schedule puts its Sullivan-city part in the St. Louis, MO-IL HMFA and lists Crawford again in the Missouri non-metropolitan schedule — while `configuration/white_labels/mo.py` stores one value, `"Crawford County"`, and `_get_entity_id` carries no sub-county disambiguation. It resolves correctly regardless: `fmr/listCounties` returns exactly one whole-county row per county, and HUD names split parts separately ("Sullivan part", "Sullivan city part of Crawford"), neither of which can match `"Crawford County"` under exact equality. **Bedroom size (family unit size).** One bedroom per two persons **within each generation**, capped at four. HAKC's subsidy standard is a base ratio plus five exceptions, not a household-size lookup: § 982.402(b)(1) requires only "the smallest number of bedrooms needed to house a family without overcrowding" and leaves the standard to each PHA. Two of the five exceptions are the ordinary case rather than an edge — different generations receive separate bedrooms, and persons of the opposite sex other than spouses and children under 5 receive separate bedrooms. **The word "separate" is an inference on the generations limb, not a quotation.** HAKC's bullet reads in full "Persons of different generations will receive bedrooms."; the plan omits the adjective its four neighbouring bullets all carry ("allocated separate bedrooms", "occupy the same bedroom"), and without it the bullet states no rule at all. The separate-bedroom reading comes from that parallel construction and from the chart below, which only makes sense if some rule pushes a household above the bottom of its range. HAKC's chart gives **overlapping** ranges — 1 bedroom for 1–2 persons, 2 for 2–4, 3 for 3–6, 4 for 4–8, 5 for 6–10 — so household size alone does not determine the voucher size, and a size-only lookup returns the bottom of every range.

**Generation is the one exception the screener can evaluate**, and it is read from `relationship` (`configuration/white_labels/base.py`, `relationship_options`, benefits-api `origin/main`).

| Generation | `relationship` values |
|---|---|
| +2 | `grandParent` |
| +1 | `parent`, `fosterParent`, `stepParent` |
| 0 | head of household, `spouse`, `domesticPartner`, `sisterOrBrother`, `stepSisterOrBrother`, `boyfriendOrGirlfriend`, `roommate`, `relatedOther` |
| −1 | `child`, `stepChild`, `fosterChild` |
| −2 | `grandChild` |

```
bedrooms = min(4, sum(ceil(members_in_generation / 2) for each occupied generation))
```

A single-person household is 1BR, which the ratio and HAKC's fifth exception both give.

**Every input maps, including the ones that carry no generation.** The table covers all fifteen `relationship_options` values at benefits-api `origin/main`, plus the head, who has no `relationship` row. Three carry no real generational distance — `relatedOther` ("aunt, uncle, cousin, in-law") genuinely spans generations, and `roommate` and `boyfriendOrGirlfriend` describe a living arrangement — and a fourth case is a null or unrecognised `relationship`, which `HouseholdMember.relationship` permits (`screener/models.py`, `blank=True, null=True`; the serializer does not require it). **All four fall to the head's generation**, the reading that yields the lower count. Scenario 24 pins `roommate` at generation 0; it does **not** pin the fallback. Because the table names all fifteen values explicitly, the only input reaching the fallback is a null or unrecognised `relationship` — which the frontend's `relationshipToHH` validator makes unenterable (`src/Components/Steps/HouseholdMembers/utils/schema.ts`, `createHouseholdMemberSchema` and `createBasicInfoPageSchema`, benefits-calculator `origin/main`), leaving that branch reachable only through the API. The default is written down anyway because the alternative is an invisible answer, not a wrong one: an unmapped member either drops out of the count silently or raises, and a raise reaches the error contract's second arm and returns not eligible, $0 — see Missing Eligibility Criteria 16.

**The four-bedroom cap is MFB's, not HAKC's, and it is load-bearing.** The generation split can sum above four without needing five generations: head, spouse and three others at generation 0 (five members → 3BR) plus three children (→ 2BR) give five bedrooms at household size eight, across two generations. Five *occupied* generations is the smallest household to reach it, at size five, since `relationship` spans exactly five. `_validate_bedrooms` raises `HudIncomeClientError` outside 0–4 (`integrations/clients/hud_income_limits/client.py`, `_validate_bedrooms`, benefits-api `origin/main`), and both `household_eligible` and `household_value` must catch it — so an uncapped count would present that household as **not eligible, $0**, the same silent failure this spec flags for St. Louis City and for household sizes above 8. Capping before the client call turns it into a known understatement instead. HUD publishes no FMR above four bedrooms; HAKC's own chart runs to five. At five persons the cap is also HAKC's answer: its chart starts the 5BR band at six persons and puts a five-person family in the 4–8 band. Recorded as Missing Eligibility Criteria 17, and Scenario 21 pins it.

**A pregnant sole applicant is a two-person family, for the bedroom count only.** § 982.402(b)(5) requires a family of a pregnant woman with no other persons to be treated as two persons. `pregnant` is collected per member (`screener/models.py`, `HouseholdMember.pregnant`, benefits-api `origin/main`) and the head is reachable through `Screen.get_head()`, so unlike the other § 982.402(b) family-unit-size rules this one is observable and is modelled. Where `household_size` is 1 and the head is `pregnant`, a notional second member is added **in the child generation**, giving `⌈1/2⌉ + ⌈1/2⌉ = 2` bedrooms rather than 1. § 982.402(b)'s lead-in scopes the paragraph to family unit size, so the rule moves the bedroom lookup and nothing else: the income-limit comparison still uses the real `household_size` of 1, and the notional member is not a dependent and earns no $480 deduction. Three siblings implement it the same way — `KsHcv`, `IlHcv` and `WaHcv` each carry an `_effective_household_size` returning 2 for a pregnant sole head, and `IlHcv`'s docstring records that it applies the rule even though its own bedroom map makes it value-neutral, "so a future map change stays correct" (`programs/programs/white_labels/{ks,il,wa}/hcv/calculator.py`, `_effective_household_size`, benefits-api `origin/main`). Missouri is that map change: under a composition rule the notional member's generation decides the count, so the rule is no longer value-neutral here. Scenario 20 pins it.

Applied to the thirty-seven Test Scenarios below, this rule gives the same bedroom count as a plain size lookup everywhere except Scenarios 12, 20, 22, 29, 31 and 34 — 12, 29, 31 and 34 because their two members sit in two generations, 20 because the notional member added by § 982.402(b)(5) sits in a second generation, and 22 because its five members occupy four. Scenario 21 is the one household where the two agree only because of the cap: the composition rule returns five bedrooms and a size lookup four, and `min(4, …)` lands both at four. The count can only rise, never fall: splitting a household by generation never merges anyone.

An agency assigning bedrooms differently — a different ratio, or a different exception set — would move every value in the payment-standard table above, since the bedroom count is what selects a column from it.

**The annual value floors at $1, not $0.** `benefits-calculator`'s results page filters on `programValue(program) > 0`, so a program computing exactly $0 is removed from results entirely. **Rounding.** § 5.628(a) requires nearest-dollar rounding but does not break an exact $0.50 tie; the HUD-50058 Form Conventions section resolves it as round-half-up. **Deductions.** § 5.611(a) sets four mandatory deductions and this spec models all four; the block below is the complete definition of adjusted income. The first two are flat: **$480 for each dependent**, and **$400 once** if the head, co-head or spouse is 62 or over or has a disability. A dependent is any member other than the head, **co-head** or spouse who is under 18, a full-time student, or has a disability; a `fosterChild`-relationship member never counts, which Scenario 29 pins. The co-head is in that list by MFB convention rather than by § 5.603's words, which say only "other than the family head or spouse" — MFB has no co-head field, `domesticPartner` stands in for one, and both `il_hcv` and `ks_hcv` exclude it from the dependent count through the same `HEAD_RELATIONSHIPS` tuple that sets the elderly-or-disabled flag. HUD's § 5.403 definitions read "head (including co-head)", so the convention follows the part's usage elsewhere. The direction is **narrowing**: it withholds $480 from a household whose domestic partner is disabled or a full-time student. Scenario 28 pins it. The elderly/disabled deduction is a family-level flag taken once, not a per-member count; Scenario 30 pins that cardinality, and is the only scenario where two head-relationship members qualify at once.

**These are the pre-HOTMA amounts, in force at HAKC.** Like the $50 minimum rent and the bedroom standard, this is one agency's position applied statewide: the HOTMA clock is per-PHA and PHAs could set a compliance date from 2024 onward, so any of the other 44 Missouri agencies already past its own date uses $500/$550, a ten-percent (a)(3) floor, a $52,787 imputation threshold and a live § 5.618 asset limit. Only HAKC's plan was read. HUD enforces HOTMA sections 102 and 104 for all non-MTW, non-FRS PHAs from 2027-01-01, and until a PHA reaches that date HUD's inflation-adjusted values table does not apply to it — the table says so in its own applicability note, naming the annual inflationary adjustments among the 102/104 provisions. HAKC's plan is organized around that split and is unambiguous about which side it is on: Chapter 6.A, "applicable prior to the PHA's HOTMA 102/104 compliance date," carries $480 and $400; Chapter 6.B, "Income and Subsidy Determinations under HOTMA 102/104," carries $525. Missing Eligibility Criteria 4 rests on the same fact from the same plan. Chapter 6.A therefore governs, and the $500/$550 pair in HUD's CY2026 table applies to a PHA already past its date. At the compliance date the figures become whichever calendar year's table is current then, so the switchover is a maintenance item rather than modelled here.

**HOTMA runs on two clocks, and every figure in this spec is keyed to one of them.** PIH 2026-15's Appendix A gives five provisions an early compliance date that has already passed, and puts everything else in sections 102 and 104 at 2027-01-01. Taking a figure from the wrong side of that line is the easiest way to misstate this program, so the split is stated once here and each affected rule points back to it. The 2027-01-01 date attaches to HUD-50058 transactions effective on or after it rather than to a switch thrown that morning: the same notice tells PHAs running a 120-day reexamination lead to begin collecting and verifying income under HOTMA policies from September 2026. That does not move the figures below, which state what a household would be assessed on today under HAKC's current plan. It does mean the pair a household is eventually admitted on is likely to be Chapter 6.B's, since HAKC's own average wait is three years.

**§ 5.611(a)(3) and (a)(4) — the health/medical and childcare deductions.** Three of the four limbs are modelled and the fourth is declined, for the reasons below. HAKC's plan splits (a)(3) into two deductions with *different* qualifying tests — § 6-II.D health and medical care, for (a)(3)(i), and § 6-II.E disability assistance expenses, for (a)(3)(ii) — and they share the three-percent floor. The (a)(3) figures are modelled on the pre-HOTMA text, the text HAKC's Chapter 6.A reproduces in full, for the same reason the $480/$400 pair is: the increase of the (a)(3) threshold from three percent to ten percent is itself a sections 102/104 provision, on the same 2027-01-01 clock.

```
annual_gross      = countable annual income (the same figure the income test uses)

# (a)(3)(i) — health and medical care. Family-level gate, three-percent floor.
medical           = Screen.calc_expenses("yearly", ["medical"])
deduction_a3i     = max(0, medical − 0.03 × annual_gross) if elderly_or_disabled_family else 0

# (a)(4) — childcare. The cap depends on which activity the care enables, and it is
# taken on the employment income *included in annual income* — so each member's
# figure is what the three person-scoped § 5.609(b) limbs leave behind, never the
# raw earned total. A member those limbs zero is not an earner at all.
childcare         = Screen.calc_expenses("yearly", ["childCare"])
countable_earned  = a member's earned income after § 5.609(b)(3), (b)(8) and (b)(14)
                    — the per-member figure Criterion 1 aggregates
earners           = [countable_earned(m) for m in household if countable_earned(m) > 0]
deduction_a4      = min(childcare, min(earners)) if earners else childcare

adjusted income   = max(0, annual_gross − $480 × dependents − $400 if elderly/disabled
                    family − deduction_a3i − deduction_a4)
```

**The floor at zero on adjusted income is load-bearing in Missouri and nowhere else.** `IlHcv._adjusted_income` and `KsHcv._adjusted_income` both `return max(Decimal(0), …)` under the docstring "Annual income less the two modelled mandatory deductions, floored at zero (24 CFR 5.611(a))" (`programs/programs/white_labels/{il,ks}/hcv/calculator.py`, `_adjusted_income`, benefits-api `origin/main`), and Missouri follows them. In the siblings only the two flat deductions can reach it; here (a)(3)(i) and (a)(4) can exceed income outright, which Scenario 36 does. The floor does not change that scenario's value — a negative 30 percent prong still loses the `max` to the $50 minimum rent — No scenario value rests on it, so it must be written into the formula — nothing would flag its absence.

**(a)(3)(ii), attendant care and auxiliary apparatus, is not modelled.** Its qualifying test is nothing like (a)(3)(i)'s. HAKC states four conditions: the expenses must "(1) … be necessary to enable a family member 18 years or older to work, (2) … not paid to a family member or reimbursed by an outside source, (3) in combination with any medical expenses, exceed three percent of annual income, and (4) … not exceed the earned income received by the family member who is enabled to work." The screener can test (3) and can approximate (4), but it collects nothing that identifies care as attendant care, nothing that establishes it enables a particular member to work, and no field distinguishing paid-to-a-family-member. The nearest field, the `dependentCare` expense leaf, is **residual non-child dependent care** — it sits alongside `childCare` and `childSupport` under the same category and carries no disability meaning — so mapping it to this limb would deduct ordinary adult-dependent care under a disability rule. Recorded as Missing Eligibility Criteria 11 rather than modelled on a field that does not mean what the rule needs.

**The (a)(4) cap applies to the work branch only, which is why it is conditional above.** HAKC § 6-II.F: "When a family member looks for work or furthers their education, there is no cap on the amount that may be deducted for childcare – although the care must still be necessary and reasonable. However, when childcare enables a family member to work, the deduction is capped". A household reporting no earned income cannot be on the work branch, so no cap applies to it. Where there *are* earners, HAKC caps by a single member's income and picks the lowest-paid: "When more than one family member works during a given period, the PHA generally will limit allowable childcare expenses to the earned income of the lowest-paid member." That is `min(earners)`, not the sum — the sum would be materially more generous than the plan allows, and **Scenarios 35 and 37** are the households that separate them: 35 deducts $12,000 where a cap at the sum would deduct the full $18,000, and 37 deducts $480 where the sum would deduct the full $3,000. Scenario 32 does not: its second earning member's wages are excluded before the list is built, so the list has one entry and the two readings agree.

**The earner list is built after the § 5.609(b) exclusions, not before.** HAKC's plan fixes the cap to the employment income that **is included in annual income**, so a member whose employment income the exclusions remove is not an earner and sets no cap. Three limbs can do that: (b)(3) zeroes an under-18 earner who is not the head or spouse, (b)(8) zeroes a `fosterChild`, and (b)(14) reduces a dependent full-time student to the $480 dependent-deduction amount. Building the list from a raw per-member `calc_gross_income` call — which applies only the income-*type* exclusion and none of the three — understates the deduction, and in its sharper arm caps a household whose only earner is an excluded minor where the rule leaves it uncapped. Both arms **narrow**, which is why no eligibility answer moves and the error is invisible in the value alone. Scenario 32 pins the case where a limb zeroes the member outright; **Scenario 37** pins the case (b)(14) creates and the other two limbs cannot — a member left *partly* included, at $480, who is still an earner and sets the cap there.

That direction is conservative, not neutral.

**The `fosterChild` dependent row is the one a dev is most likely to reverse, and it reverses toward the majority.** Missouri follows § 5.603's text, which excludes foster children from the definition of dependent. Ages are derived from `birth_year_month` via `calc_age()` against the pinned reference date, never from the raw `age` field.

**Silent degradation.** `get_screen_payment_standard` falls through to the metro FMR with no exception, no log and no flag when either the household's ZIP is absent from the SAFMR table or the `area_name` string fails to match — so a HUD rename would downgrade every ZIP in the metro at once. The program `year` must be set at deploy for the calculator to compute a period at all.

**The error contract has three arms, not one.** `screener/views.py` catches only `DependencyError` around a program's evaluation, so anything else raised here takes down the whole white label's eligibility response. `ks_hcv` — the pattern Missouri should follow — guards all three cases in both `household_eligible` and `household_value` (`programs/programs/white_labels/ks/hcv/calculator.py`, `KsHcv.household_eligible` and `KsHcv.household_value`, benefits-api `origin/main`):

1. `except HudIncomeClientError` → not eligible, and $0 **unfloored**. This is the expected arm: the HUD client raises it when the API is down, the county cannot be matched, the year is unconfigured, or `household_size` falls outside 1–8 (`_validate_household_size`, `integrations/clients/hud_income_limits/client.py`, `_validate_household_size`, benefits-api `origin/main`).
2. `except Exception` → the same degradation, with `logger.exception`. Not redundant: it is what keeps an unexpected bug in one program from returning a 500 for every program on the screen, and the log is what keeps it from being silent.
3. A `None` `household_size` is checked **before** the HUD call and returns early rather than raising, because there is no limit to compare against. Normally unreachable — a null `household_size` is a missing dependency and the program is not calculated at all — but the guard is there.

**Household sizes above 8 are the same arm as a HUD outage.** HUD publishes income limits for sizes 1 to 8 only, so a household of 9 or more raises `HudIncomeClientError` and is shown as not eligible — a correct-looking wrong answer, since such a household is not ineligible but unmeasurable. This spec models that behaviour and does not extrapolate the 8-person limit.

## Test Scenarios

**Ages are as of a 2026-09-28 reference date.** `calc_age()` compares only year and month — `age_from_date` returns `today.year − birth.year` when `today.month >= birth.month` and one less otherwise (`screener/models.py`, `age_from_date`, benefits-api `origin/main`) — and `get_reference_date()` returns the current date, so a stated age holds for the twelve months from the member's birth month. Four scenarios turn on an exact age (14 at 62, 15 at 18, and 26 and 27 at 17); all four use a September birth month so the boundary is reached in the reference month itself. **All four stop being boundary cases in September 2027**, when `age_from_date` rolls them to 63, 19 and 18 — and none fails when that happens, because 63 is still elderly, a 19-year-old who is not a student is still not a dependent, and an 18-year-old head's income counts under § 5.609(a)(1) exactly as a 17-year-old head's does, so all four keep returning their stated verdicts while no longer killing the `age > 62`, dependent-shortcut and minor-head mutations they were written for. Re-pin the three birth years at the first re-verification after that date; the scenarios go quietly uncovered rather than red. **Five scenarios are age-dependent and do not fail quietly**, and they run on different clocks. Scenario 31's student turns 18 in **January 2028**, at which point § 5.609(b)(3) stops reaching them, § 5.609(b)(14) caps their wages at $480 and the scenario's own stated value moves from $2,064 to $1,920 — so it goes red rather than inert, which is why its birth month is January and not September. Scenarios 32, 33, 34 and 36 each carry a 16-year-old earner born June 2010, and all four turn on § 5.609(b)(3) reaching that member: in **June 2028** their wages count, and the stated values move to $5,064, $9,564, $5,760 and $9,684 (Scenario 34 loses its dependent deduction as well, and its three-percent floor rises with the larger base; Scenario 36 loses its dependent deduction and its TTP passes back to the 30 percent prong). All five go red on their dates rather than quietly uncovered, so no re-pinning is needed before then; re-derive them at the first re-verification after June 2028.

**Known scenario gaps**

- Criterion 2 (`household_size >= 1`) has no scenario: it is true of every screen the frontend can submit, so it is not falsifiable and no scenario would fail if it were removed.
- No scenario tests a data gap's **unmodelled** branch: each is a rule the screener cannot evaluate, and a test asserting the assumed branch would only restate the assumption. Three gaps carry a *modelled* half that is covered, which is a different thing: Gap 16's generations and single-person exceptions (Scenarios 12 and 22, and every one-person scenario), Gap 17's four-bedroom cap (Scenario 21), and the § 982.402(b)(5) limb Gap 20 records as **not** a gap (Scenario 20). Gaps 14 (the payment-standard percentage) and 21 (the payment-standard vintage) are the exceptions in kind rather than in treatment — every scenario value moves with them, so no scenario can isolate either.
- The $50 minimum-rent floor is exercised as the binding TTP component by **Scenario 36**, the suite's only household where neither income percentage reaches it. Every other modelled income level produces a higher income-based TTP. Such a household is possible because both prongs run on § 5.609(b)-**countable** income: one with substantial *excluded* income sits below them while being perfectly able to pay $50. It is not exempt either. § 5.630(b) requires inability to pay **together with** a situational trigger (lost or pending assistance eligibility, eviction for nonpayment of the minimum rent, a decrease in income including loss of employment, a death in the family), and such a household has none. The exemption itself stays unmodelled, for households that do have a trigger — Missing Eligibility Criteria 19.
- No scenario exercises the three exceptions to HAKC's subsidy standard that this spec does not model — the opposite-sex rule, the under-5 rule and the live-in-aide bedroom — each of which depends on a fact the screener does not hold (Missing Eligibility Criteria 16). The two exceptions the spec does model are covered: the generations exception by Scenarios 12 and 22, and the single-person exception by every one-person scenario, where the base ratio yields the same 1BR.
- Every modelled § 5.628(a) component now has a scenario in which it is the binding figure: the 30 percent prong in 31 of the 33 valued scenarios, the 10 percent prong in Scenario 33 — which is also the only scenario able to pin that prong's income base — and the $50 minimum rent in Scenario 36.
- § 5.611(a)(4)'s cap is exercised in all four of its arms — uncapped (33), capped with one included earner (17, 32), capped where the **lowest-paid** earner and the sum differ (35), and capped by a member whose earnings are *partly* excluded (37), where § 5.609(b)(14) leaves a dependent full-time student $480 of included earned income and the cap lands there rather than at the head's wage.
- No scenario reports a `dependentCare` expense, which is correct rather than a gap: § 5.611(a)(3)(ii) is not modelled (Missing Eligibility Criteria 11), so that leaf feeds nothing.
- No scenario distinguishes the two TTP rounding orders pinned in Benefit Value. At $480 and $400, rounding monthly income to the dollar before taking 30 percent gives the same TTP as rounding once, for all thirty-three valued scenarios; a scenario that separates them would need 30 percent of the exact monthly figure to land on the half-dollar while the monthly figure's own cents round down.
- No scenario exercises a **split county** — one whose territory HUD divides between two areas. Crawford is Missouri's (Benefit Value, "A second county is split between two HUD areas"). There is no branch to test: `fmr/listCounties` returns one row per county and HUD names the split-off part separately, so a stored `"Crawford County"` resolves to exactly one entity. The guard is the county-resolution test, not a scenario.
- No scenario exercises the `HudIncomeClientError` degradation path (Benefit Value, "Silent degradation"). Exercising it needs a mocked HUD client response rather than a household scenario.
- Household size 6 has no scenario; the bedroom rule is exercised at household sizes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8, which covers every distinct bedroom count it produces (1BR through 4BR) and both routes to 4BR — reached on composition (22) and reached by the cap (21).
- No scenario reaches the **generation-less fallback** — the branch that catches a `relationship` that is null or absent from the generation table. It is not enterable: the frontend requires `relationshipToHH` as a non-empty string (`src/Components/Steps/HouseholdMembers/utils/schema.ts`, the `relationshipToHH` validator in `createHouseholdMemberSchema` and `createBasicInfoPageSchema`, benefits-calculator `origin/main`), and the table names all fifteen `relationship_options` values, so no screen can produce an unmapped member. `HouseholdMember.relationship` is nonetheless `blank=True, null=True` (`screener/models.py`, benefits-api `origin/main`) and the serializer does not require it, so the branch is reachable through the API and is kept. Scenario 24 exercises `roommate`, which the table places at generation 0 explicitly — a different assertion.
- Generation −2 (`grandChild`) is the one row of the generation table no scenario exercises. Adding a grandchild to Scenario 22 would push its split to five and reintroduce the cap as a confound, and the bucket travels the same code path as any other distinct generation, so the gap is left open deliberately rather than covered by a scenario that would test two things at once. Generations +2, +1, 0 and −1 are exercised by Scenario 22 and the rest of the suite.
- The disability route into the elderly-or-disabled deduction is exercised by Scenario 28 alone, through `member.has_disability()` on a co-head; Scenarios 10, 14, 16 and 30 all reach the same flag by age. § 5.611(a)(3)'s family-level gate is that flag, so it is covered by age (Scenarios 16 and 34) and not by disability — no scenario combines a disabled head with reported medical expenses.
- Scenario 11's legal-status gate is evaluated at `config` scope, not by calculator branch logic; no scenario exercises the mixed-family exception (Data Gap 7) that leaves a mixed-status household's HAP unprorated, because that is a deliberate `assumed-met` gap rather than a testable branch.
